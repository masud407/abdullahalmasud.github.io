Spectro-Temporal Analysis of Doppler Twinkling Artifacts

Investigating the RF and IQ signal characteristics that distinguish color Doppler twinkling in PMMA markers from non-twinkling metal targets — through temporal variation, wall filtering, and spectral analysis.

View Resources Signal Pipeline Key Findings

Overview

What This Study Examines

Color Doppler twinkling can improve ultrasound visualization of highly reflective targets, yet the signal characteristics responsible for the artifact remain incompletely understood. This project examines RF and IQ signals to identify the temporal and frequency-domain features that distinguish twinkling.

Motivation

Why It Matters

Identifying what makes PMMA twinkling markers behave differently from stable metal reflectors informs both artifact characterization and potential clinical applications — enabling more precise use of twinkling as a diagnostic or marker-detection tool in ultrasound imaging.

Signal-Processing Pipeline

From Raw Channel Data to Doppler Estimation

Raw channel RF data were processed through beamforming, IQ demodulation, wall filtering, and Doppler estimation — each stage designed to isolate twinkling signal characteristics.

Figure 1 — Signal-processing framework showing the full pipeline from raw RF acquisition through beamforming, IQ demodulation, wall filtering, and final Doppler estimation.

Temporal Analysis

Pulse-to-Pulse Signal Variation

PMMA demonstrated greater temporal variation than metal across repeated pulses and frames. The metal response remained comparatively stable, while the twinkling signal showed pronounced changes — a core distinguishing characteristic of the artifact.

Figure 2 — Beamformed RF signal analysis comparing PMMA and metal targets. PMMA shows pronounced temporal variation across pulses; metal remains stable.

Frequency-Domain Analysis

Spectral Content Before and After Wall Filtering

Beamformed RF signals were analyzed in the frequency domain at both stages of wall filtering. PMMA produced broader and more irregular spectral content, whereas metal maintained a smoother and more stable spectral response across conditions.

Figure 3 — RF frequency spectra for PMMA and metal targets before and after wall filtering. PMMA shows broader, more irregular spectral energy compared to the stable metal response.

IQ Spectral Analysis

Post-Demodulation Spectral Differences

IQ analysis revealed comparable spectral differences after demodulation. PMMA showed greater spectral variation across the IQ frequency range, while metal maintained a narrower and more consistent frequency response.

Figure 4 — IQ frequency spectra showing spectral differences between PMMA and metal after IQ demodulation. Spectral variation in PMMA persists post-demodulation.

Key Findings

What the Data Shows

  • 01 PMMA showed greater temporal signal variability than metal across repeated pulses and frames.
  • 02 Wall filtering revealed clear signal differences between twinkling PMMA and stable metal reflectors.
  • 03 PMMA produced broader and more irregular RF spectral content compared to metal.
  • 04 Spectral differences between targets persisted after amplitude normalization.
  • 05 IQ analysis confirmed similar spectral distinctions following demodulation.
  • 06 Temporal and frequency-domain features provide complementary measures of the twinkling response.
